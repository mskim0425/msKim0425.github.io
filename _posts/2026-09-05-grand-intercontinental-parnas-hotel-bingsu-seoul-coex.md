## Three Bingsu, Three Days, One Summer Flex

Seoul's luxury hotels compete every summer over **bingsu (빙수)** — Korean shaved ice — and it's become its own dessert culture. I had three days in a row around COEX before summer ran out, so I did the obvious thing: **one bingsu a day, all three of the Summer Premium Bingsu** at the **Grand InterContinental Seoul Parnas Lobby Lounge & Bar**.

Short version: hotel bingsu tastes *clean*. Nothing is cloying or aggressive; every element feels deliberate. A nice touch — **each time a bingsu arrives, the server gives a short explanation** of what's in it and how to eat it, which makes the whole thing feel like a small tasting rather than just dessert. It is expensive — but if you want to flex a little and spend a summer afternoon somewhere genuinely refined, this is the one.

---

## 1. Jewel Tomato Bingsu (주얼 토마토 빙수) — ₩75,000

My pick of the three, and the prettiest thing on the menu. It arrives in a traditional **mother-of-pearl (자개) box** on a lacquered octagonal tray — lift the butterfly-inlaid lid and there's a jewel-like mound of glazed cherry tomatoes over milk ice, with yuzu syrup, tomato sherbet, and tomato-yuzu jam.

![Jewel tomato bingsu in a traditional mother-of-pearl box at InterContinental Parnas](/images/parnas-bingsu/tomato-bingsu.webp)
_Glazed "jewel" tomatoes in a mother-of-pearl box. This is the essence of Korean plating._

Full disclosure: I don't love cherry tomatoes — I ate far too many of them while dieting. And yet this was **bright and refreshing**. Most desserts like this leave a sugary film in your mouth or sit heavy afterward; this one leaves nothing but a clean, citrusy finish. The yuzu does a lot of work.

![Jewel tomato bingsu with lamp light at the Lobby Lounge](/images/parnas-bingsu/tomato-lamp.webp)
_Under the lounge lamplight. If you're here for the photos, this is the one._

![Spoonful of tomato bingsu with milk ice and coconut flakes](/images/parnas-bingsu/tomato-spoon.webp)
_Tomato sherbet and milk ice — light, zesty, and nothing like a cherry tomato salad._

## 2. Jeju Apple Mango Bingsu (제주 애플망고 빙수) — ₩120,000

The showstopper. A brass cone heaped with cubes of sweet **Jeju apple mango** over milk ice, with mango compote and mango pearls hidden inside. On top sits a perfect little "mango" — actually **Valrhona Opalys white chocolate** shaped and colored like the fruit.

![Jeju apple mango bingsu with a Valrhona chocolate mango on top at InterContinental Parnas](/images/parnas-bingsu/mango-bingsu.webp)
_Cubes of Jeju apple mango piled over milk ice — the "mango" on top is Valrhona Opalys chocolate._

I usually leave decorative pieces on the plate. Not this one. Cut it open and the chocolate shell gives way to a mango center wrapped in a filling that's not ice cream but somehow **creamier than whipped cream** — an elegant, refined sweetness that never gets heavy.

![Valrhona chocolate mango cut open showing the creamy mango center](/images/parnas-bingsu/mango-inside.webp)
_The Opalys "mango" cut open: chocolate shell, mango compote center, and a filling creamier than whipped cream._

---

## 3. Signature Red Bean Bingsu (시그니처 팥빙수) — ₩75,000

The classic, done properly. Milk ice under a generous layer of **homemade Korean red bean**, cubes of red bean yokan, nut granules, and a scoop of **mugwort (쑥) ice cream** crowned with a mugwort tuile. Condensed milk is served on the side so you can tune the sweetness.

![Signature red bean bingsu with mugwort ice cream and red bean yokan at InterContinental Parnas](/images/parnas-bingsu/red-bean-bingsu.webp)
_Homemade red bean, mugwort ice cream, yokan cubes, and a mugwort tuile — built for a Korean palate._

The mugwort is unmistakable — a strong, herbal note — but it never crosses into heavy. It's clearly aimed at Korean tastes, and it works: the yokan is excellent, and the condensed milk gives you a second, sweeter register to play with.

---

## Bonus: The Limited Moon Jar Cake (달항아리 케이크) — ₩23,000

If you're lucky, add the **moon jar cake** — a matte white dome shaped like a Korean *dalhangari* (moon jar), dotted with cornflower petals and a fleck of gold, with the hotel's chocolate plaque beside it. It's part of a **collaboration with moon-jar painter Choi Young-wook (최영욱)**, runs only through **mid-September**, and the lounge makes just **six plates a day** — we happened to catch one.

![Moon jar (dalhangari) shaped white cake with cornflower petals at InterContinental Parnas](/images/parnas-bingsu/moon-jar-cake.webp)
_The moon jar cake: a matte white dome on celadon, finished with cornflower petals and gold leaf._

Slice it and there's a fruit compote center over a thin sponge base under the white mousse shell. Taste-wise it reminded me of the fresh cream cake at **Una House (우나하우스)** — light, milky, and not too sweet.

![Moon jar cake cut in half showing the fruit center and sponge base](/images/parnas-bingsu/moon-jar-cut.webp)
_Inside the moon jar: fruit compote over sponge under a white mousse shell._

## The Lounge: Crystals and Live Jazz

The Lobby Lounge & Bar is the hotel's signature space. Overhead hangs artist **Park Seon-gi's "Column of Lights"** — ten huge columns made of some 60,000 crystals, like a galaxy pouring from the ceiling. From **Monday to Saturday** there's a **live jazz and pop ensemble** (piano, saxophone, double bass, clarinet, trumpet, flute) in four evening sessions: 19:20–20:00, 20:20–21:00, 21:20–22:00, and 22:20–23:00 (no performances on Sundays; lineup may change). If you can, time a bingsu for the early-evening set.

## FAQ

**Q: What bingsu does Grand InterContinental Seoul Parnas serve?**
A: Three Summer Premium Bingsu at the 1F Lobby Lounge & Bar: Jewel Tomato served in a mother-of-pearl box (₩75,000), Jeju Apple Mango (₩120,000), and Signature Red Bean with mugwort ice cream (₩75,000). Each is sized for two people.

**Q: How much is the hotel bingsu at InterContinental Parnas?**
A: ₩120,000 for the Jeju apple mango, ₩75,000 for the tomato or red bean — service charge and tax included. Note that the Parnas voucher can't be used for this offer.

**Q: When is the summer bingsu available?**
A: July 1 to September 30, 2026, from 12:00 to 22:00, at the Lobby Lounge & Bar on the 1st floor. The lounge itself is open 09:00–24:00 daily.

**Q: Which one should I order?**
A: The jewel tomato is my favorite — light, refreshing, and stunningly plated. The Jeju apple mango is the showstopper (and the priciest); the signature red bean is the classic Korean choice. If you can, do what I did and try all three.

**Q: Do I need a reservation, and is parking available?**
A: It's walk-in with 136 seats, so weekdays are easy; weekends usually mean a wait. Parking is straightforward and validated for lounge guests — at the Parnas building only.

**Q: Is there live music?**
A: Yes — a live jazz and pop ensemble plays Monday to Saturday in four evening sessions from 19:20 to 23:00 (no performances on Sundays; subject to change).

**Q: How do I get to the InterContinental Parnas Lobby Lounge?**
A: Take Line 2 to Samseong Station (삼성역); the hotel connects directly through COEX. The Lobby Lounge & Bar is on the 1st floor.
